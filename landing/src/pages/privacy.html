The short version

  • Your incidents, notes and evidence are encrypted and stored on your device. We cannot read them.
  • We only hold your email address if you give it to us (waitlist), encrypted backups if you make one, and encrypted share links if you create them. There are no accounts.
  • If you choose to draft a document with AI, only the fields you select are sent, to create that draft.
  • No ads, no third-party trackers, no selling or renting data. Ever.

Your record stays on your device

The incidents you log, the people you name, your notes, impact statements, wellbeing check-ins and the files in your evidence vault are stored in your browser's storage on your device. Before anything is stored, it is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a random key, which is itself locked with your passcode (and, if you add them, your passkey or recovery phrase). The key is kept in memory only while the app is unlocked. We never receive your passcode, your recovery phrase, your key or the unencrypted contents of your record.

Because we cannot read your record, we cannot recover it if you forget your PIN and have no backup, and we cannot hand its contents to anyone, including your employer.

Encrypted backup

Backup is off by default. When you turn it on, your record is encrypted on your device and only the encrypted file is uploaded. It is protected by a recovery phrase that only you hold. We can see that a backup exists, its size and when it was made; we cannot see what is inside. Backups are identified by a code derived from your phrase, not by your name or email. You can delete the online copy at any time in Settings.

Sharing with a lawyer

If you create a share link, the incidents and files you choose are encrypted on your device with a new random key before upload. The key is only in the link itself, after the # sign, which browsers never send to us, so we cannot open the copy. Links expire after 24 hours or 7 days, and you can turn one off at any time, which deletes the copy at once. Each time a link is opened we record the time and a general description of the browser (no IP address), so you can see when it was used. Expired copies are deleted, and the record of the link is removed 30 days later.

Reminders

Reminders are off by default. If you turn them on, your browser gives us a push address, and we store it with the time and days you chose. Reminders contain no text from us: your device decides what the notification says, and can disguise it. Your browser's push service (for example Google, Apple or Mozilla) delivers it and can see that a reminder was sent, but not your record. Turning reminders off deletes the push address.

Analytics and cookies

This website uses Plausible Analytics, which counts page views without cookies and without collecting personal data. The web app does not use advertising or tracking cookies. It uses essential storage on your device to hold your encrypted record and settings, and a security cookie that protects forms from forgery.
